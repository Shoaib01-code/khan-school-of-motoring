Your Route to ADI

Three parts. We train you for the two that decide it.

To become an Approved Driving Instructor you pass three qualifying tests. Part 1 is theory. Parts 2 and 3 are the practical tests — and they're where proper, structured training makes the difference.

A computer-based test of the Highway Code, driving theory and hazard perception. Most PDIs come to us with Part 1 done — or just getting started.

Who Can Apply

Thinking about it? Check the basics.

The DVSA sets the entry requirements for the ADI register. In general, you need to:

  • Be 21 or over
  • Have held a full driving licence for at least 3 years
  • Pass an enhanced criminal record (DBS) check
  • Pass the three qualifying tests — Parts 1, 2 and 3

Questions

Instructor training — common questions

What's the difference between a PDI and an ADI?

A PDI (Potential Driving Instructor) is a trainee working through the qualifying tests. An ADI (Approved Driving Instructor) has passed all three parts and is on the DVSA register — legally able to charge for driving tuition. Our training takes you from PDI to ADI.

Do I need Part 1 passed before I talk to you?

No. Some trainees come to us before Part 1, most come after it. Either way, we'll look at where you are and tell you the most direct route from there.

Why does Part 3 catch so many people out?

Part 3 doesn't test your driving — it tests your teaching: how you plan a lesson, manage risk and give feedback, in the structured way the examiner needs to see. It's a learnable skill, but it has to be taught properly — which is exactly what our training focuses on.

What does ORDIT registered mean?

ORDIT is the DVSA's official register of driving instructor trainers. Training with an ORDIT-registered school means your trainer meets the standard the DVSA itself sets for teaching instructors — it's a credential you can verify, not just a claim.

How much does instructor training cost?

It depends on where you're starting from and how much training you need — message us on WhatsApp or call, tell us your situation, and we'll give you a straight answer with no obligation.
